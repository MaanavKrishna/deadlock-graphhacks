# Deadlock after the hackathon

## First customer hypothesis

A trusted community of small creative agencies or freelancers with complementary spare capacity. Start with one existing network; do not launch an empty general marketplace. The initial buyer hypothesis is a community operator who already makes member introductions.

## Product value to validate

Can a multi-party agreement unlock useful work that members could not arrange through a direct introduction? Do participants understand the terms, trust their counterparties, and complete a second exchange? Is negotiating the whole cycle worth the additional coordination?

## First experiment

Interview five community operators and ten potential participants. Ask for recent concrete examples of idle capacity and unmet needs, how introductions happen today, and why exchanges fail. With explicit permission and appropriate privacy controls, manually arrange a small number of real proposals before building broad integrations.

Keep three separate measurements: proposals accepted, work actually delivered, and repeat exchanges. A graph match or synthetic credit total is not revenue or realized value.

## Pricing hypotheses

Test an operator subscription for a private exchange workspace, then evaluate a completed-exchange fee if there is a legitimate fulfillment and dispute process. These are experiments, not validated willingness to pay. Avoid introducing a credit currency or payment custody in the initial product.

## Most consequential risks

- Not enough compatible offers and needs in a small community.
- Mismatched expectations about quality or the value of an hour.
- One late participant delays the entire cycle.
- Participants prefer a simple introduction or paid subcontracting.
- The operator's coordination work costs more than the exchange creates.

## Next product increments only after evidence

Authenticated agency workspaces; concrete deliverable descriptions; milestone scheduling; explicit expiry and cancellation terms; reputation based on completed work; approval notifications; coordinator tools; private tenant graphs; targeted transaction updates and distributed concurrency control. Payments and enforceable agreements require separate product and professional review.

## Competitive position

Business barter is established. The proposed differentiator is agents assembling and negotiating specific connected agreements with inspectable constraints and history. Validate that this is valuable against direct referrals and existing barter networks rather than claiming a new category or guaranteed moat.
